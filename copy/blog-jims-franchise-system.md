# Why I Don't Ask You to Take My Word for It

For over twelve years I was obsessed with an idea a hundred different "gurus" online were all selling — build websites, run ads, do SEO for other businesses, and get paid well for barely any work. I bought the courses. I did the self-guided tutorials. I learned how to set up, build and rank websites, and run paid advertising on Facebook and Google.

The truth is, the whole system never felt real to me. The results were uncertain. There was no guarantee you were actually doing a business a service, rather than taking their money and throwing mud at the wall to see what stuck. Even when something worked, it could feel like magic — which bit actually did it? The ad? The keyword? The landing page? You're rarely sure, and neither is the client.

I couldn't commit to offering people hope, taking their money, and not being able to guarantee I could deliver. I hadn't achieved that kind of success for myself. I wasn't going to promise it to someone else.

Then I found Jim's.

If you live in Australia, you'll know the business operated under the Jim's banner. It started with Jim's Mowing and grew into a franchise behemoth covering so many service industries it's become a meme — "there's a Jim's for that."

My fencing business was one of those franchises. I worked in and with the franchise structure through the worst of it — the worldwide shutdown, the pandemic. All the while I learned and studied the setup and model of Jim's. In my opinion, the service industry itself is only a fraction of their success.

They provide a system for operators to become their own business owners. Teach them the standard required to get the results. Then give them access to the greatest marketing system in the country, focused on the service industry.

For a while I thought I didn't need it. There was so much work coming in, and they were charging a fortune for those leads — not clients, leads. Quote checkers, time wasters, people who just wanted to know how much it would cost.

It nearly cost me my business. Learning that system, the hard way, is what eventually brought me to build LIP Services.

Their centralised call centre sells those leads. Those qualified leads feed the franchisees with enough work to keep everyone busy.

The brand is a big part of the marketing setup. Selling those leads to their own franchisees — an internal marketplace — feeds the demand for franchise growth and pays for the creation of more leads. The effect is almost perpetual, and profitable.

## What can we take away from this?

The first layer is brand identity. It's a huge factor in being found, even sought out. A large call centre and an instant messaging system distributing those leads straight to the operators who want them is more than just clever.

The second layer is the education within the brand. Every franchisee is taught to respond quickly, quote quickly, and do a great job that exceeds expectations. The bottom line: do what you say you will. Be specific on the phone, follow up with a text, keep the customer informed, and have every detail of the job recorded and specified. Remove any objection or criticism before it can develop.

To be fair, this isn't rocket science. But I found out it isn't common practice either.

It's often time-consuming, and it interrupts work and social time. You feel like you always need to be working. The phone call after dinner. The quote request just as you're back on the tools. Inconsequential in isolation — but when it happens twice a day, or four times a week, time can feel like it's always slipping away.

## How does knowing this affect your business?

Jim's has grown over many years and built a lead management and deployment system that a small business couldn't replicate on its own. Until now.

### Phase 1 — Be found

What if you could put your perfect client in front of niche-specific content, information and advice? That's brand building for a local business — and it takes work and consistency, not randomness.

One of the simplest ways to do it today is social media. Keep it simple: talk about the job you're doing. Two or three raw videos a week, out on site or in the workshop, explaining something people always get wrong. Random facts, repairs, or issues you've run into.

### Phase 2 — Be contactable

Remember those phone calls? Late at night, making you want to switch your phone off after work. Ringing while you're up a ladder, carrying something heavy, or mid-conversation with a client?

If you can't answer promptly like a call centre can, you may have lost that lead before you even knew you had one.

This is where AI voice agents come in. Trained on your business, they sound real, give real information about your services and offers, and guide a call you couldn't take into a booked appointment — complete with a follow-up text and email to confirm it. That's engagement, not just an answering machine.

### Phase 3 — The follow-up

When was the last time you had time to properly follow up on a lead? Send an email reminding them why they needed you. Send an SMS to check they received the quote. Remind them you're still there, checking their plans, when most others aren't.

This is where the real backend work happens — integrating the voice agent and booking platform with a CRM for fully automated follow-ups. A quote goes out, four automated emails and texts follow to make sure it isn't missed or lost. Someone calls but isn't ready to book — that lead goes into an automated nurture sequence instead of disappearing.

It's often said it takes somewhere between 7 and 20 touch points before someone trusts you enough to buy. Send them to your socials, your videos, your testimonials, your Google reviews. Warming a lead is a slow business — but it's rarely done properly in the service industry, even though you've probably experienced it plenty of times dealing with an online business yourself.

### Phase 4 — Get paid

Have a system in place to follow up on the ones who haven't paid yet. This is a real problem for a lot of businesses. You've done what you promised — but not everyone pays on time.

It's rarely because someone's being dishonest. More often it's just missed in the daily grind, and a gentle reminder is all it takes to get an explanation and a timeline. Late payments are exactly the cash flow your business needs to survive, so they're always going to worry you.

The same automation used for leads and follow-ups in Phase 3 can be pointed at overdue accounts. Your accounting package flags them so you're aware — the system does the leg work of following up so your invoice doesn't get forgotten.

## What would that give you back?

Time. The ability to spend more of your day doing the work you actually started your business to do. After-hours that are actually yours again.
